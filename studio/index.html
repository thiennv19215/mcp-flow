Step 1

Cast

Make a character once and every scene can reuse it. Flow draws the portrait from your description, then locks that exact design into any frame or clip that references it.

Or attach a picture instead of describing them:

For a description, Flow makes this many portrait options and uses the first for the saved character. An attached image creates one character from that image.

Step 2

Frames free

Still images: storyboard frames, or the first and last frame of a clip. Turn on Match previous frame so each frame inherits the one before it — that is what keeps a character identical across a set.

Frames

Step 3

Shots

Video clips. Give a shot a first and last frame and Flow animates between them; chain a shot onto the one before it for a continuous move. Flow charges by length — the estimate is live at the bottom.

Shots

Carry on from a clip you already made

Picks up on the real last frame of a finished clip, so the two play as one unbroken take with no visible cut. Flow makes you choose one of two things: an exact join, where the person's look comes from that frame itself, or a locked character, where the face stays put but the opening frame is only close. It cannot do both at once.

Step 4

Voice

Record narration for free, at any length. Google AI Studio gives you the same voices Flow uses and takes a delivery note; macOS voice speaks with a voice installed on this Mac and needs no key. Neither spends credits.

Optional

Restyle

Change an existing clip without reshooting it: relight it, change the weather or time of day, remove or restyle something. Flow shows no price before an edit, and a 4-second clip cost 20 credits when measured.

Step 5
